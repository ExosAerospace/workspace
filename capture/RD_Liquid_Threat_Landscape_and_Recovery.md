# R&D Brief — Global Liquid/Non-Solid Threat Landscape + US Recovery-Uniqueness Case

**Prepared for:** John Quinn, Exos Aerospace · **Date:** 2026-08-24 · **Classification:** Proprietary / Unclassified — open-source only
**Purpose:** Build the case for the MDA reusable-liquid-target program on two pillars: **(1)** solids are NOT the only threat we must protect against — the liquid threat spans SRBM to strategic ICBM and is now going *reusable*; **(2)** no other US provider can fly a **ballistic threat profile AND recover** the vehicle. Grounds `MDA_Reusable_Liquid_Target_Capture.md` and the RFI response. *This is the initial R&D pass — expandable.*

## Pillar 1 — Solids aren't the only game: the liquid threat spans the whole ladder

The highest-consequence strategic threats to the US homeland — the heaviest, longest-range ICBMs — are **liquid-fueled**. Liquid is not a legacy footnote; it is the top of the threat pyramid *and* the bottom (SCUD).

**Strategic (ICBM) tier — liquid-fueled, can strike CONUS:**
- **North Korea — Hwasong-17 (KN-28):** two-stage **liquid** ICBM, est. range ~15,000 km — **the largest road-mobile liquid-fueled ICBM in the world** (confirmed test 18 Nov 2022). Joined by liquid **Hwasong-14/-15**, **-16/KN-27**, and the newly unveiled **Hwasong-20** (Sep 2025). North Korea's ICBM leg is overwhelmingly **liquid**.
- **Russia — RS-28 Sarmat ("SATAN 2"):** **liquid**, super-heavy, MIRV ICBM (~11,000–18,000 km), up to 10 heavy / 15 light warheads or **24 Yu-74 hypersonic glide vehicles** + countermeasures — replacing the liquid R-36. Legacy R-36/UR-100 also liquid.
- **China — DF-5 / DF-5A (CSS-4):** **liquid** ICBM, 12,000–13,000+ km, can strike the western/continental US; DF-4 (liquid, retiring). Liquid heavy ICBMs alongside China's solid road-mobile force.

**Theater tier — liquid SRBM/MRBM/IRBM (the SCUD family, per the SCUD brief):**
- **SCUD-B/C/D** (liquid, 300–700 km); **No-Dong** (liquid MRBM ~1,000–1,300 km); **Shahab-1/2/3** (Iran, liquid); **Hwasong-5/6/7** (NK). Iran holds the **largest missile stockpile in the region** (ODNI, Mar 2025); NASIC catalogs 14+ Iranian variants.

**Takeaway:** a target fleet that is **overwhelmingly solid-motor cannot present the plume/optical/IR signature of the liquid systems that dominate both ends of the threat ladder** — from SCUD to Sarmat. That is a structural, high-consequence test-fidelity gap.

## Pillar 1b — The emerging vector: **reusable liquid** vehicles (orbital-capable = ICBM-range energy)

A new class is proliferating fast — **reusable, liquid-fueled, orbital-class vehicles** — and any orbital-capable system carries ICBM-range energy. China's commercial-military fusion is the leading edge:
- **LandSpace Zhuque-3 (ZQ-3):** reusable, **LOX/methane**, stainless-steel, Falcon-9-class (76.6 m, ~660 t). **First commercial land recovery of an orbital-class booster, 19 Aug 2026** (maiden recovery *failed* Dec 2025 — see Pillar 3). Reusable to ~20 flights.
- **A whole ecosystem behind it:** Galactic Energy Pallas-1, Deep Blue Nebula-1, Orienspace Gravity-2, Space Pioneer Tianlong-3, **iSpace Hyperbola-3** (with China's first drone recovery ship). State-backed reusability R&D funding.

**Takeaway:** the threat is not only liquid — it is becoming **reusable and rapidly reconstitutable**, which means higher launch cadence to defend against. A reusable *liquid target* is the only cost-matched way to test at that cadence. **Solids represent neither the signature nor the tempo of this emerging class.**

## Pillar 2 — US competitive landscape: who can fly a threat profile AND recover it?

The specific capability — **vertically-launched, ballistic threat-representative trajectory, liquid signature, AND vehicle recovery/reuse** — is **unoccupied in the US.** The nearest players each miss on one axis:

| US provider | Flies threat *ballistic* profile? | Liquid ballistic signature? | Recovers / reusable? | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Exos (BLK3→BLK4)** | **Yes** (lofted ballistic, SCUD-family) | **Yes** (liquid; storable roadmap) | **Target capability (3–5-vehicle R&D)** | The niche |
| **Stratolaunch Talon-A** | No — **air-launched winged hypersonic glide** (Mach 5+) | No (glide vehicle, not a ballistic-missile plume) | **Yes** — runway recovery, 10+ flights | Wrong regime (glide, not ballistic target) |
| **Rocket Lab HASTE** | Yes (suborbital ballistic) | Partial (expendable Electron) | **No — expendable** | No recovery/reuse |
| **Blue Origin New Shepard** | No — **fixed vertical hop** | No (not threat-representative) | Yes (vertical) | Wrong profile; tourism/payload, not a target |
| **Kratos Erinyes / legacy targets** | Yes | No (solid) | No — expendable | Solid + expendable |

**Honest read:** the US *does* now have reusable recoverable fast vehicles — **Stratolaunch has genuinely solved reusable hypersonic recovery** (first since the X-15). But that is a **runway-landing winged glider in the hypersonic-cruise regime**, not a **vertically-launched ballistic-trajectory liquid target**. Recovering a vehicle **on a ballistic reentry trajectory** is a *distinct, unsolved-in-this-class* problem. So Jon's claim holds precisely: **no one else in the US flies the ballistic threat profiles with a liquid signature and has a path to recover on that trajectory.** That is Exos's defensible white space.

## Pillar 3 — Recovery is hard R&D: expect to expend 3–5 vehicles before first recovery

Recovering a vehicle on a ballistic reentry trajectory is a genuine development effort, not a given — and the open-source record supports Jon's **3–5-vehicle** expectation:
- **LandSpace Zhuque-3** failed its first-stage recovery on the **Dec 2025 maiden flight**, then succeeded **Aug 2026** — one public failure before success, on a state-backed program.
- **SpaceX** expended numerous boosters over years before routine recovery.
- The US has had **no recoverable vehicle in the ballistic/hypersonic class between the X-15 (1960s) and Stratolaunch (2025)** — underscoring the difficulty.

**Program implication (fold into the $75M plan):** treat recovery as a funded **R&D sub-phase with expected attrition of 3–5 vehicles before first successful recovery.** This is honest, fundable (MDA understands flight-test attrition), and it is *why* the reusable-target payoff is defensible — competitors haven't paid this cost for a ballistic liquid target. The early expended vehicles still deliver value as **expendable threat targets** during the recovery-development campaign.

---

## Bottom line for the MDA case

1. **Threat:** liquid dominates both ends of the ladder (SCUD → Sarmat/Hwasong-17/DF-5) and is now going **reusable** — solids represent neither.
2. **Gap:** MDA's solid target fleet cannot present liquid signatures at the needed cadence.
3. **Uniqueness:** no US provider flies a **ballistic liquid threat profile and recovers** it — Stratolaunch (glide/recover) and HASTE (ballistic/expendable) each miss an axis.
4. **Honesty:** recovery is real R&D — **plan for 3–5 vehicles to first recovery**; expended units serve as targets meanwhile.
