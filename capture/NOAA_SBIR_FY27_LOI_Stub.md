### Abstract (250–300 words — revise to NOFO topic text)

Exos Aerospace proposes the Suborbital Atmospheric Contaminant Sampling System (SACSS), a reusable instrument mounted on the FAA-licensed BLK3 Precision Suborbital Reusable Launch Vehicle (PSRLV) that opens and seals sampling chambers at three altitude bands (40–60, 60–80, and 80–100 km) on both ascent and descent, returning intact physical samples for laboratory-grade composition analysis (EM-EDX, ICP-MS). SACSS directly addresses the measurement gap identified as an urgent priority by the AIRL Working Group whitepaper (ESSC/ESF, Zenodo 20831147, Jun 2026) and the gap at the core of NOAA's Earth's Radiation Budget (ERB) Initiative: reentry-generated alumina, metallic aerosol, and metal oxides are injected into the 40–100 km zone — entirely above the WB-57 aircraft ceiling (~19 km) and below single-use sounding rockets — with no standing in-situ physical-sample-return capability. NOAA's own modelers (Maloney, Portmann, Rosenlof et al., JGR 2024) have identified altitude-resolved composition measurements as the critical unknown driving radiative forcing uncertainty in the middle atmosphere.

Phase I delivers: (1) SACSS instrument system requirements; (2) substrate and sample-preservation protocol study (Collicott/Purdue sub, EM-EDX and ICP-MS requirements); (3) detailed chamber open/seal mechanism design; (4) ground-test prototype fabrication; (5) validation testing; and (6) a Phase II flight-integration and campaign plan targeting the first 40–100 km composition dataset.

Exos retains SBIR data rights (private-expense development); NOAA, FAA, and constellation operators are anchor customers for the resulting data product. Phase II delivers a multi-flight campaign at ~$250K/flight — a fraction of sounding-rocket costs — scalable to ~8 flights/yr for ongoing monitoring.
