# EXOS INTEGRATED CONSORTIUM (EIC) — Prime-Integrator Model
*How a lean company fields a 200-person prime team — without any partner giving up autonomy.*

## The idea
Exos acts as the **prime integrator**. For any opportunity too big for one small firm, Exos knits 3–4 specialized companies into a single **virtual prime** that bids **Firm-Fixed-Price (FFP)**. Each partner quotes and owns its own scope; Exos holds the contract, integrates the whole, and carries the **contingency/backup plan** so no single-partner failure can sink delivery. The customer buys **one accountable prime**; the partners keep their independence and win work none could bid alone.

## How it works (the flow)
1. Exos identifies the pursuit and decomposes it into clean, **ownable scopes**.
2. Each partner **quotes its own scope FFP** — prices its own risk, no forced cost-share.
3. Exos assembles the integrated FFP bid, adds systems engineering / program management / integration, and prices the **prime margin + contingency**.
4. On award, **Exos holds the prime contract**; partners execute under **FFP task orders / subcontracts**.
5. Exos maintains a **2-deep backup** for every critical scope — self-perform or swap a qualified alternate if a partner slips — protecting the FFP promise to the customer.

## What each partner gets (autonomy + group benefit)
- Owns and prices its **own scope**; risk **bounded to that scope** (no joint-and-several exposure).
- Keeps its **background IP, brand, and other customers** (non-exclusive except on the shared pursuit).
- **Non-circumvention / non-solicitation** — Exos won't cut you out; you won't go around Exos on that deal.
- Access to bids requiring **scale, certifications, and past-performance** the partner lacks alone.
- **Backup coverage both ways:** if you're overloaded, the bench absorbs surge without costing you the award.

## What Exos brings (why it holds the prime)
- The **contract, customer relationship, and the capture & proposal** machine.
- **Systems engineering + program management** that make N companies read as one team.
- The **compliance boundary** — ITAR/CUI on GCC High via Atomus, CMMC — so small partners don't each build it.
- The **single-point-of-failure contingency**: a qualified alternate bench and the plan to execute it. This is Exos's value-add and the basis of its integration margin.

## The vehicles (pick per pursuit — light to heavy)
- **Teaming Agreement** (pre-award, default, lowest friction) → **FFP Subcontract / Task Order** on award.
- **Standing Consortium MOU** — one master agreement so repeat bids don't re-paper each time.
- **Joint Venture (JV)** — a formal joint entity only when a specific large program or set-aside requires it.
- **Technology License** — when a partner's IP is embedded in an Exos product.

## The easy structure (so a small company can sign without a legal department)
- **One 2-page master** per partner: MNDA + background/foreground **IP split** + **non-circumvention** + **scope-bounded FFP** + defined **exclusivity window** + clean **exit**.
- **One 1-page work order** per pursuit: scope, price, schedule, and named **backup**.
- That's the whole footprint. Everything else lives in the FFP task orders on award.

## Governance (lightweight)
- **Exos = Consortium Lead / Program Manager** (single throat to the customer).
- Per-pursuit **capture lead** + a simple go/no-go and bid-review gate.
- Money is clean: each partner keeps its FFP; **Exos takes an integration fee / prime margin** and funds the contingency bench.

## Bottom line
Exos becomes the **integrator that lets small companies bid like a prime** — FFP, accountable, and de-risked by design. Partners stay independent; together they take work no one of them could touch.
