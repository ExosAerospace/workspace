Hello John,

Thanks for kicking this off and for keeping it efficient. Here is my input on the survey, plus one suggestion on how we keep this committee from becoming another charter that sits on a shelf.

On how to run the survey:
1. Keep it short and structured. Aim for 10 to 12 questions, mostly ranking and multi-select, that a member can finish in about 10 minutes. Prose-only surveys hand us a pile of opinions we cannot compare. Structured answers we can rank and act on.
2. Run it in two quick rounds, a light Delphi. Round 1 diverges, where we collect scope candidates and desired outcomes. Round 2 converges, where we hand back the consolidated list and members rank priorities. That is how we reach real consensus on scope instead of the loudest voice winning.
3. Use MS Forms or Google Forms, attributed, with an option to submit scope opinions anonymously so people are candid about boundaries. Field it for one week. Then I would bring a draft charter with an already-prioritized scope to the next meeting rather than more open discussion.

Proposed questions, mapped to charter sections so the results assemble themselves:
1. In one sentence, why should LVRS exist, and who is the primary beneficiary?
2. Scope inclusions, rank by priority: ELVs; RLVs; upper stages; reusable upper stages; in-space servicing, mobility and logistics and refueling (SM&L, ISAM); hypersonic suborbital rockets; rocket-powered hypersonic aircraft; airbreathing concepts; cross-cutting operability, reliability and safety.
3. Explicit exclusions: what is deliberately out of scope? Constraining the problem matters as much as defining it.
4. Interfaces: which existing committees or activities do we overlap, and where is the boundary?
5. Deliverables: what tangible products should this committee produce in the next 12 to 24 months, for example conference sessions, a technology roadmap, standards or policy input, workshops, or white papers?
6. Impact measure: how will we know a year from now that this committee mattered?
7. Cadence and structure: meeting frequency, and do we need subcommittees?
8. Gaps: who or what discipline is missing from the table?

On scope, my vote, and it echoes much of what Jess raised:
Define a core, then interfaces, rather than trying to own everything. Core is launch vehicles, both ELV and RLV, and entry systems, including upper stages with an explicit step toward reusable upper stages, SM&L, and refueling. That is the through-line, and it is where the reusability, operability and safety technologies actually compound. Then interface, do not absorb, on airbreathing and rocket-powered hypersonic aircraft. The common-technology argument Jess makes is real, but pulling those fully inside costs us the constraint. Naming them as interfaces keeps the door open without diluting the charter. I would also make cross-cutting operability, reliability and safety of RLVs and ELVs an explicit standing theme, since that is the connective tissue across every concept on the list.

One thing I want to push, and it is the reason I am in this group:
Charters usually fail on follow-through, not on scope. I suggest we build a lightweight tracking method into the charter from day one, an objectives ledger where every committee objective carries an owner, a deliverable, a due date, and a quarterly status. It is one shared page. It is the difference between a group that produces two named sessions and a roadmap next year and one that produces another round of minutes. I am happy to stand that up and maintain it if the group wants it.
