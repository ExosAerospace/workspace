# LVRS Charter Working Group — Round 1 Survey (Forms-ready first pass)

**Prepared by:** J. Quinn (Exos Aerospace) for the LVRS Charter Working Group
**Date:** 2026-08-25
**Purpose:** Round 1 of a light two-round Delphi. This round *diverges* — it collects scope candidates, desired outcomes, and boundary opinions. Round 2 hands back the consolidated list for members to *rank*, and a prioritized draft charter comes to the next meeting instead of another open discussion.
**Platform:** Build in MS Forms or Google Forms. Attributed by default, with an anonymous free-text option on the scope-boundary questions (Q3/Q4) so people are candid.
**Length target:** 12 questions, about 10 minutes. Mostly ranking and multi-select.
**Field for:** one week from send.

> Build notes for whoever stands up the form are in *[italics/brackets]* and should not be pasted into the live form.

---

**Intro text (paste at top of form):**

This short survey shapes the LVRS Technical Committee charter. It takes about 10 minutes and is mostly ranking and multi-select. This is Round 1 — we are collecting the full set of scope candidates and desired outcomes. In Round 2 we will hand back a consolidated list for you to prioritize, then bring a draft charter to the next meeting. Where a question offers an anonymous option, feel free to use it so we get candid input on boundaries. Thank you for helping keep this charter sharp rather than shelf-bound.

**Q1. Purpose (short answer — required).**
In one sentence: why should LVRS exist, and who is the primary beneficiary?
*[Long-answer text, one line expected.]*

**Q2. Scope inclusions — rank by priority (ranking — required).**
Rank these from highest to lowest priority for the committee to own:
- Expendable launch vehicles (ELVs)
- Reusable launch vehicles (RLVs)
- Upper stages (expendable)
- Reusable upper stages
- In-space servicing, mobility & logistics, and refueling (SM&L / ISAM)
- Entry, descent, and recovery systems
- Hypersonic suborbital rockets (rocket-powered boost/test platforms)
- Rocket-powered hypersonic aircraft
- Airbreathing hypersonic concepts
- Cross-cutting operability, reliability, and safety
*[MS Forms ranking question. If ranking of 10 items feels heavy, fall back to "select your top 5" multi-select.]*

**Q3. Own vs. interface (multi-select grid — required).**
For each area below, mark whether LVRS should OWN it, treat it as an INTERFACE (coordinate, do not absorb), or leave it OUT.
Rows: ELVs · RLVs · upper stages · reusable upper stages · SM&L/ISAM/refueling · entry & recovery · hypersonic suborbital rockets · rocket-powered hypersonic aircraft · airbreathing hypersonic · operability/reliability/safety.
Columns: Own · Interface · Out.
*[Likert/choice grid. This is the question that actually resolves the "core vs. absorb-everything" debate.]*

**Q4. Explicit exclusions (long answer — optional, anonymous allowed).**
What should be deliberately OUT of scope? Constraining the problem matters as much as defining it.
*[Provide an "answer anonymously" note here.]*

**Q5. Interfaces and boundaries (long answer — optional).**
Which existing AIAA committees or activities do we overlap, and where should the boundary sit (for example propulsion TCs, hypersonic/high-speed airbreathing, space logistics/ISAM, spaceports, range & safety, GNC)?

**Q6. Deliverables — select all that this committee should produce in the next 12–24 months (multi-select — required).**
- Named technical sessions / panels at SciTech, AVIATION, ASCEND
- A launch/entry + reusable-upper-stage technology roadmap
- Standards input
- Policy / regulatory input
- Workshops
- White papers
- Lessons-learned / best-practice capture (operability, reliability, safety)
- STEM / university engagement
- Other (specify)

**Q7. Top deliverable (single choice — required).**
Of the items you selected in Q6, which ONE matters most in the first 12 months?
*[Single-choice mirroring Q6 options. Forces a lead priority.]*

**Q8. Impact measure (short answer — required).**
How will we know, one year from now, that this committee mattered? Name one concrete signal.

**Q9. Cadence (single choice — required).**
How often should the full committee meet?
- Monthly · Every 6 weeks · Quarterly · Twice a year · Other

**Q10. Structure (multi-select — required).**
Do we need standing subcommittees? Select any that should exist:
- Reusable upper stages / SM&L
- Operability, reliability & safety (standing theme)
- Roadmap & standards
- Hypersonic interface / liaison
- STEM & workforce
- None — keep it a single body
- Other (specify)

**Q11. Membership & participation (single choice + short answer — required).**
*[Directly answers Linton Honda's 8/24 cadence question and the 10-of-50 turnout, and tests the AIAA norm Amrutur Anilkumar cited on 8/24 — annual review, 3-year member term, continuation at the pleasure of TC leadership. Framed opportunity-first, per John Vinson's 8/24 steer: step up activity to create participation before any roster pruning; roster changes run through the normal annual recruiting cycle (revisited Sept for 2027).]*
Follow-up A (single choice — the lead question): what most improves participation?
- More ways to contribute (subcommittees to form/chair, real deliverables) · A stricter roster/attendance standard · Both, in that order (opportunities first) · Other
How often should the membership roster be reviewed and refreshed?
- Quarterly · Annually (AIAA norm) · Annually, with a light mid-year participation check · Other
Follow-up B (single choice): the AIAA norm is a member term of up to three years, renewable at TC leadership's discretion. Adopt as-is, shorten, or lengthen?
- Adopt the 3-year term as-is · Shorter term · Longer term · No fixed term · Other
Follow-up C (short answer): if we ever need one, what should the standard be for an "active" member (for example, attends at least half of meetings or contributes to one deliverable/subcommittee per year)? Note this would inform the annual/September review only, not mid-cycle removal.

**Q12. Gaps (long answer — required).**
