Exos Aerospace — Defense Industrial Base Funding Strategy

A layered, low-dilution capital stack across four sources — DPA Title III · DoD Office of Strategic Capital · Texas SEARF · Ainigma (RTO/FNEX) — with the Exos Integrated Consortium as the teaming stack, anchored to the Navy's defense-industrial-base build-out.  |  Proprietary / Unclassified  |  August 23, 2026

1. The play

The DoD and Navy are pouring money into expanding the domestic defense industrial base — and three of our four target sources exist specifically to fund industrial-capacity build-out, not R&D papers. That is the lane we fit. Exos, one of only three FAA-licensed reusable rocket launch providers in the US, plus Type-5 composite cryogenic tanks from partner Scorpius Space Launch Company (SSLC) — TRL 9, flew as fuel/LOX tanks on Intuitive Machines' Odysseus and Athena — which Exos integrates and sells as SSLC's channel, plus the Exos Integrated Consortium (prime-integrator bench: Scorpius, MATECH, Super Carbon, Hybron, CDT, Starfighters, Atomus, Albers, Southwest Composite Works — all US/ITAR, 2-deep backups), is a domestic, reusable flight-test and responsive-space industrial capability the government can fund into existence.

The move is to stack the four sources so each layer de-risks the next and minimizes equity dilution: put a non-dilutive federal grant and low-cost federal debt underneath the private raise, use Texas money as the match that unlocks the federal money, and let a Navy demand signal anchor the whole case.

3. Source-by-source playbook

① DPA TITLE III — non-dilutive industrial-base grant
  • What it is: DoD's authority (50 USC 4501–4568) to create/expand/protect domestic sources for critical components and technology. FY25 ran ~28 grants ($125K–$203M, ~$5.2M avg); the FY27 request scales this account toward ~$30B. Actively funding the composites, solid-rocket-motor, and hypersonics industrial base right now.
  • Exos fit: Strongest anchor is domestic Type-5 linerless composite cryogenic tank capacity with partner SSLC as the tank producer and Exos as prime/integrator and sales channel — composites (incl. ultra-high-temp) is an active Title III sector, and in-space cryo storage/refueling is a national priority. Secondary anchors: reusable suborbital flight-test infrastructure and propulsion (RDRE) hosting/flight-test as a domestic capability.
  • The ask: A project award to expand domestic Type-5 cryo-tank capacity (SSLC as producer; Exos as prime/integrator) and/or reusable flight-test capacity — framed as closing an industrial-base gap.
  • Door: businessdefense.gov (Industrial Base Analysis & Sustainment / IBAS + DPA Title III office); watch for project-specific NOFOs/BAAs. Requires a Presidential determination for the capability area — the Navy demand signal (§4) helps make that case.
  • Gotcha: Title III funds a capability the government has said it needs — lead with the requirement, not the technology.
② OFFICE OF STRATEGIC CAPITAL (OSC) — low-cost federal debt
  • What it is: DoD office (statutory via FY24 NDAA) issuing direct loans and loan guarantees of $10M–$150M across 30+ covered technology categories that explicitly include space launch, spacecraft, and advanced materials. Purpose: crowd in private capital and bridge the "valley of death" from lab to full-scale production. FY27 request scales the program toward ~$20–30B.
  • Exos fit: Finance the capex to industrialize — facility, tooling, and equipment for IPS/tank integration, RDRE production/test build-out, or BLK4 production — as low-cost debt instead of dilutive equity.
  • The ask: A direct loan sized to the production-line capex, repaid from flight-services / tank / contract revenue.
  • Door: cto.mil/osc — OSC credit program (Equipment Finance / capital loan program). Also the SBIC-CT initiative (licensed private funds that co-invest alongside OSC) — a route where Ainigma or a partner fund could pair with federal capital.
  • Underwriting reality (not an early-stage problem): OSC underwrites like a lender — the test is repayment capacity / debt-service coverage on the specific capex loan, not company age. Exos meets this from strength: an 11-year-old, revenue-generating prime, one of only three FAA-licensed reusable rocket providers in the US, with access to partner SSLC's Type-5 composite cryogenic tanks flown to the Moon (Intuitive Machines IM-1/IM-2) through its 18% equity stake and SSLC sales-channel role. The work is simply demonstrating that the production-line capex is covered by revenue + contract backlog and sizing the loan to that coverage — present as an established operator, never as a startup.
③ TEXAS SPACE COMMISSION — SEARF (the match that unlocks the rest)
  • What it is: Texas Space Exploration & Aeronautics Research Fund — state grants, including a pathway that funds the matching-fund requirement when a company otherwise lacks the capital to capture a federal award.
  • Exos fit: The state dollars that satisfy match requirements (TACFI 1:1, SBIR, and cost-share on Title III/OSC), keeping cash and equity intact.
  • The ask: SEARF award positioned as the match that leverages multiples of federal money into Texas.
  • Door: Warm path via Norman Garza (Exec Dir); congressional letters of support already in motion. Tie the request to jobs/industrial base in Texas (Greenville final assembly & test).
  • Gotcha: Keep the ask framed as leverage/match, not standalone R&D.
④ AINIGMA — the go-public / strategic-capital leg
  • What it is: Ainigma is the reverse-takeover (RTO) path to a public listing via FNEX (a registered FINRA broker-dealer; DD cleared, onsite 8/20). Exos holds ~95% at close, giving Exos continuous public-market access and stock as acquisition currency on top of the cash raise.
  • Role in the stack: anchors the equity base the OSC debt sits on, can co-invest alongside OSC/SBIC-CT to amplify federal leverage, and supplies the acquisition currency to roll up bench partners (e.g. Southwest Composite Works, CDT) into the domestic industrial capability the DIB case describes.
  • Status: DD cleared; RTO/PIPE sequencing underway alongside MDB Capital (Nasdaq IB) and the Reg D 504 bridge.
★ THE EXOS INTEGRATED CONSORTIUM (EIC) — the DIB capability, as a team
  • The model: Exos is the prime integrator holding the contract, integration, flight vehicle, and the single-point-of-failure contingency bench. Each partner FFP-quotes its own scope; Exos owns the compliance boundary (ITAR/CUI/CMMC via Atomus). This turns a set of individual companies into one fundable, resilient domestic industrial capability — which is precisely what Title III and OSC are built to stand up.
  • Why it strengthens every federal application: a US/ITAR-clean bench with 2-deep backups on every critical part (CMC → Ultramet; carbon-carbon → C-CAT; composites → Rock West) directly answers the government’s core DIB worry — fragile, single-source, or foreign-exposed supply chains.
  • Starfighters Space (NYSE American: FJET) sits in this bench as the air-launch / hypersonic flight-test leg (seven F-104s at NASA KSC; STARLAUNCH), widening the combined flight-test & responsive-space offering.

4. The Navy DIB demand signal — the anchor

Title III and OSC fund capabilities the government has said it needs. The Navy's interest in building out the defense industrial base is the demand-pull that converts our applications from "nice technology" to "funded requirement." The move:

  • Convert the Navy interest into a written statement of need / demand letter (or requirement reference) for domestic reusable flight-test + responsive-space + cryogenic-tank capacity.
  • Attach that demand signal to the Title III project pitch and the OSC loan case as the "why now / who buys."
  • Use it to support the Presidential-determination case for the Title III capability area.

6. Risks & guardrails

  • OSC underwriting: lead with Exos's 11-year operating history, revenue, flight heritage, and contract backlog to show debt-service coverage on the capex loan; size the loan to that coverage. This is a financial-structuring point, not a maturity question.
  • Title III needs a determination: the area must be authorized — the Navy demand signal is how we support that.
  • Accuracy discipline: RDRE is hosted / flight-tested, not Exos-developed (hardware path is a trade-study down-select, GTL vs NASA MSFC license); BLK3 is FAA-licensed (Mar 2026) but has not flown commercially; Exos has flown since 2018. Keep all gov-facing claims to this standard.
  • Export/ITAR: controlled technical data stays out of any foreign-touch path; screen before release.
