*Prepared for John Quinn, Exos Aerospace — 2026-08-26. Reflects Dr. Kelly's confirmed commitment. Finalized dual-institution structure: University of Washington = research institution of record; Purdue = subaward (suborbital methods); Exos = small business concern and prime applicant.*

## 1. The play in one paragraph
Exos and the University of Washington submit an NIH STTR (PA-27-102, September 8, 2026) to develop and validate BLK3 as a reusable suborbital microgravity platform for onset-kinetics screening of microphysiological systems (MPS) and organ-on-chip payloads before an ISS flight commitment. Dr. Edward Kelly (UW) anchors the biomedical science; Exos is the small business concern providing the flight platform, payload integration, and recovery; Purdue joins by subaward for suborbital experiment methods. Framed to NIBIB as a biomedical research platform and device.

## 2. Finalized team structure (STTR-compliant)
- **Small Business Concern (≥40% of work): Exos Aerospace** — vehicle, payload integration, flight operations, recovery. Prime applicant; holds the award and submits through eRA Commons.
- **Research Institution of Record (≥30%): University of Washington — Dr. Edward Kelly, science lead.** Kidney-chip and drug-transporter MPS; NCATS TraCe MPS kidney center (1U2CTR004867); ISS Tissue Chips flights (2019, 2021).

## 3. Why the science fits suborbital (honest framing)
- BLK3 gives ~3–4 minutes of recoverable microgravity, fast turnaround, low cost. It does **not** replace ISS sustained-microgravity science — it **screens and de-risks before** it.
- The defensible niche is **onset kinetics**: how a kidney-chip / MPS initiates its response in the first seconds-to-minutes of microgravity — the window ISS launch and transit obscure. Suborbital captures it and returns the payload intact for immediate assay.
- Guardrails held in all materials: NIH funds the PI and the science, not the rocket; suborbital microgravity is short-duration (screening, not sustained ISS science); BLK3 is FAA-licensed but **not yet flown commercially**; Exos is the platform and co-author, the PI owns the cell science.

## 4. Exos assets that make this credible
- **One of only three FAA-licensed reusable suborbital launch providers in the US.**
- **Flight-proven life-science heritage:** flew living human glioblastoma brain-tumor stem cells suborbital; results peer-reviewed in **npj Microgravity (2023) 9:92** (Quinn co-author) — documented traction, not a projection.
- Recoverable payload, ~3–4 minutes microgravity, 2-day turnaround target, standard payload integration (ICD available).
- Planned **March 2027 BLK3 flight** with available recoverable payload capacity (135 kg bay) — a concrete near-term flight to point a screening campaign at.

## 5. Specific-aims skeleton (shape around Dr. Kelly's kidney-chip MPS)
1. **Platform integration & validation [Exos lead]** — qualify a standardized BLK3 payload module for MPS/organ-chip experiments (ICD, thermal, vibration, recovery).
2. **Onset-kinetics characterization [Kelly / UW lead]** — fly the kidney-chip MPS on BLK3; characterize the 0–240 s microgravity response (transporter function, cell viability, fluid-interface behavior); generate a dataset not obtainable from ISS.
3. **Screening-pipeline demonstration [joint]** — payload prep → flight → recovery → assay → de-risk-ISS decision; establish the turnaround protocol.
