## 1. Committee Name
Launch Vehicles and Recovery Systems (LVRS) Technical Committee.

## 2. Scope

**Core (present anchor — carried from the current LVRS charter):**
To promote the development and implementation of launch vehicle and entry systems; to foster interaction and integration across technical disciplines; and to assure current, comprehensive situational awareness of commercial and government, national and international R&D efforts. The LVRS TC brings together industry, academic, and government experts within the framework and charter of the AIAA, serving as a forum for evaluating the design, development, test, and operation of reliable, affordable, and reusable launch and entry systems.

**Core scope explicitly includes:**
- Expendable launch vehicles (ELVs) and reusable launch vehicles (RLVs) — full vehicle systems, integration, and operations.
- Upper stages, with an explicit **step toward reusable upper stages** and the technologies that enable them: space mobility & logistics (SM&L), in-space servicing, assembly & manufacturing (ISAM), on-orbit / in-space refueling, and dynamic maneuver.
- Entry, descent, and recovery systems for reusable and returning vehicles.
- Hypersonic **suborbital rockets** (rocket-powered boost/sounding/test platforms), where they share vehicle, propulsion, GNC, thermal, and operations technology with launch and entry.
- **Cross-cutting operability, reliability, and safety** of ELVs, RLVs, and the concepts above — treated as a standing theme, not a sidebar, since it is the connective tissue across every vehicle class.

**Interfaces (coordinate, do not absorb):**
The following are named as interface areas so the door stays open to shared technology without diluting the charter or duplicating another committee's mandate:
- **Rocket-powered hypersonic aircraft** and **airbreathing hypersonic** concepts — strong common-technology overlap (structures, TPS, propulsion integration, operability); coordinated with the relevant Hypersonic / High-Speed Air-Breathing Propulsion committees rather than owned here.
- **Propulsion** (Liquid, Solid, Hybrid, Nuclear & Future Flight) — LVRS addresses propulsion at the vehicle-integration level; component R&D remains with the propulsion TCs.
- **Space logistics, ISAM, and spacecraft** committees — LVRS owns the launch/upper-stage/entry vehicle and its step to reusability; the on-orbit servicing mission architecture is a shared boundary.
- **Space transportation policy, spaceports, range & safety, GNC** — coordinated for standards, operability, and regulatory input.

**Explicit exclusions (constrain the problem):**
- Payload/spacecraft bus design beyond the launch/upper-stage interface.
- Air-breathing propulsion component development (interface only).
- Purely orbital on-station operations unrelated to launch, upper-stage, entry, or reusability.

### 3.a Membership & Participation
*[Added in v0.2 in response to L. Honda's question on review cadence and the low attendance at the last meeting (roughly 10 of 50 on the roster). Bracketed thresholds are for the group to set; the survey's Q11 will inform them.]*

- **Roster review cadence.** The membership roster is reviewed and refreshed **annually**, aligned to the AIAA TAD charter-review cycle, with a **light mid-year participation check** rather than a full quarterly re-vote. Rationale: quarterly turnover is too churny for an AIAA TC and creates administrative load without improving engagement; an annual refresh with a mid-year touch keeps the roster honest while remaining low-overhead.
- **Active-member standard.** A member is counted as **active** if, over the prior 12 months, they either attend at least **[half]** of scheduled meetings **or** contribute to at least one committee deliverable (session, roadmap input, white paper, review). Members who meet neither for a full cycle move to a **corresponding/affiliate** status — still on distribution, not counted toward quorum — and can return to active by re-engaging. This keeps a 50-name roster from masking a 10-person working core.
- **Quorum.** Committee business and votes require a quorum of **active** members, defined as **[a simple majority of active members]**, so decisions reflect the people actually doing the work rather than the full nominal roster.
- **Recruiting to fill gaps.** The annual review explicitly uses the survey's "gaps" input (who/what discipline is missing) to target new members, so roster refresh is additive, not just pruning.

## 4. Roles & Responsibilities

**4.a Institute (AIAA) activities**
- Organize and lead technical sessions, panels, and Forum 360 content at AIAA SciTech, AVIATION, and ASCEND; coordinate co-sponsored sessions with interface committees.
- Develop and maintain a **launch/entry & reusable-upper-stage technology roadmap**; provide standards and policy input where the community needs a common reference.
- Honors & awards oversight; best-practice and lessons-learned capture across ELV/RLV operability, reliability, and safety.

**4.b External / community activities**
- Situational awareness of commercial and government, national and international R&D — briefed back to the community.
- STEM and workforce outreach; university engagement (e.g., student design, flight-test, and payload opportunities).
- Liaison to interface committees and to external bodies (standards, range/safety, spaceport, policy).

**4.c Deliverables (next 12–24 months — proposed)**
1. Two or more named technical sessions/panels per forum cycle across the core scope.
2. A first-issue technology roadmap covering ELV/RLV + the step to reusable upper stages (SM&L / ISAM / refueling).
3. A standing operability–reliability–safety working thread with an annual state-of-the-field readout.

## 5. Objectives Ledger (proposed governance mechanism)
Every committee objective carries an **owner, deliverable, due date, and quarterly status** on one shared page. This is included in the charter from day one so the committee is measured on tangible output (named sessions, a roadmap, standards input) rather than minutes. Exos has offered to stand up and maintain the ledger for the group. The annual membership review (§3.a) reads directly off this ledger — contributing to a ledger objective is one of the two ways a member stays "active."
