**Would you use a Utah spaceport's launch or reentry facilities, should they be built?**
Yes. Exos Aerospace is one of only three FAA-licensed reusable suborbital launch companies in the United States, and the only one currently selling commercial suborbital payload services. We operate the BLK3 PSRLV, a vertical-takeoff, vertical-landing (VTVL) reusable rocket reaching 100+ km altitude with a 135 kg payload capacity. A Utah spaceport — at either the Delta or Green River candidate site — is a strong fit for Exos's VTVL suborbital mission profile (RS&H's Use Case 5), and we would use it for a mix of commercial payload flights, NASA/DoD flight-test missions, and reusable-vehicle flight operations.

**What kind of launch/reentry activity would you conduct, and how often?**
Suborbital VTVL flights carrying commercial, government, and research payloads to 100+ km. Exos's BLK3 is reusable with a target 2-day turnaround between flights (not yet demonstrated commercially), enabling a higher cadence than expendable vehicles. We would anchor a recurring flight-test and payload-services operation, not a one-off launch.

**What infrastructure or site characteristics matter most to your operations?**
Exos's VTVL platform needs minimal ground infrastructure relative to orbital vehicles — a prepared pad, power, and communications are the core requirements, without the fixed-gantry or large exclusion-zone needs of vertical orbital launch. This makes Exos a low-footprint, fast-to-activate anchor tenant capable of beginning operations early in a site's development, ahead of larger orbital tenants that need more built-out infrastructure. We would evaluate specific pad/range requirements against whichever site (Delta or Green River) the committee advances.

**Federal/commercial program fit:**
Exos has an active NASA and Department of Defense flight-test and propulsion pipeline valued at more than $50 million, and could bring existing federal program revenue into Utah's spaceport economics. This aligns with the new National Space Transportation Policy (NSPM-17, signed August 20, 2026), which directs federal support for over 1,000 annual launches/reentries and explicitly prioritizes distributing launch infrastructure beyond the coasts for national resilience — the policy driver the committee itself has cited as validating this effort.

**Timeline:**
Exos could begin site evaluation and operational planning immediately upon site selection, with an operational presence achievable within 24-36 months depending on facility buildout and licensing coordination with the FAA.

**Anything else the committee should know:**
Exos already has an executed FAA launch license — we are not a pre-license concept, we are an operating, licensed company that can make Utah's spaceport real faster than a program still working through initial licensing. We would welcome a briefing to the committee ahead of the September 22 meeting or the October public presentation.
