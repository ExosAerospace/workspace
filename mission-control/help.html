What Mission Control is

Think of it as one shared online board for everything we are working on: who is doing what, what is due, what is waiting on a decision, and where the documents live. Instead of work scattered across email, Teamwork, and people's heads, it all lives in one place everyone can see. You open it in a web browser. It is replacing Teamwork.

The big idea — you do not maintain it by hand. You keep working in email exactly as you do now. By forwarding or BCC'ing the right messages to one address (exosmc@proton.me), your everyday communication becomes the work management: Mission Control turns it into tasks, contacts, decisions, and status updates for you. No separate system to keep up, no data entry.

⚠️ Compliance — no CUI or ITAR data

At this time, do not attach documents to Mission Control, and do not BCC emails into it, that contain CUI (Controlled Unclassified Information) or ITAR-controlled data. That content lives only in its designated SharePoint folder. When in doubt, leave it out and keep it in SharePoint.

Step 3 — How to use it (the basics)

  • Your board: tasks are arranged in columns by status, for example To Do, In Progress, Done. The ones assigned to you are yours to move along.
  • Open a task: click any task to see its full details — description, due date, and notes.
  • Update a task: drag it from one column to the next as you make progress (or use the quick-move buttons), and add a note in the detail panel. That is how you "report status" — no separate update needed.
  • Add a task: use the "+ Add" bar at the top.
  • Decisions tab: anything that needs your review or sign-off shows up here. Answer it and the work moves forward.
  • Documents: attach a file directly to a task, so it lives with the work instead of buried in email.

Step 4 — The engine: your email does the work management for you

This is the heart of it. You do not log tasks or keep a system up to date by hand — your email is the input. On your normal work emails, add exosmc@proton.me. Mission Control reads who you are and what the message is about and does the filing for you — no task numbers, no codes, nothing to look up.

One address, two ways to use it:

  • BCC or forward it, with no instruction → it captures. The email is filed under the matching task, the people on it are added to Contacts, and the record is updated. Your everyday correspondence becomes the running log automatically.
  • Forward it with @Exobot plus a plain-English instruction at the top → it acts. It creates or updates your task, drafts a reply, pulls research (see the next card).
🔒 Compliance is in your hands. Only send messages you have confirmed are free of CUI and ITAR. Nothing enters Mission Control unless you send it to exosmc@proton.me — it does not read your mailbox, so you decide exactly what is captured. When in doubt, keep it in SharePoint and do not forward it.

Ask Exobot to do it — just @Exobot

Need something done? Type @Exobot and then, in plain English, the task. That is the entire trigger — everything you write after @Exobot is the instruction it carries out. No commands, no codes, no format.

  • Examples: @Exobot add a task to follow up with Marlett on Friday · @Exobot pull the latest on the AEDC solicitation and summarize it · @Exobot draft a reply to this email for my review.
  • It works the same way in chat and in email — in email, forward the message to exosmc@proton.me with @Exobot plus your instruction at the top (same address as Step 4).
  • The guardrail: Exobot does safe, internal work directly — creating and updating tasks, research, drafts, summaries, reminders — and any task or document it makes for you is assigned and attached to you. Anything external or sensitive — sending emails, government or contract submissions, financial or legal actions — it prepares and adds to your own Decisions matrix in Mission Control for your review and approval. It never sends those on its own, and approvals for your work come to you, not to anyone else.

Step 5 — NEW: turn a call into updates automatically (Transcripts)

Finished a call and have a transcript or notes? Feed it in and Mission Control does the write-up for you. Email the transcript to exosmc@proton.me with [TRANSCRIPT] in the subject line (attach the Word/text file, or paste it in the body). It will:

  • read who was on the call and add them to Contacts,
  • pull out the key points, decisions, and action items,
  • file it under the right relationship and propose an updated status and next step.

You get a short summary back and confirm before anything changes the record — so a mis-heard line never silently rewrites a deal. The transcript itself is archived for reference.

⚠️ Same compliance rule: do not submit transcripts of CUI or ITAR conversations (e.g., government technical calls). Keep those in SharePoint.

Relationship Dossiers — every relationship at a glance

The 🤝 Dossiers tab gives one card per key relationship (investors, partners, advisors, agencies): the contacts, the live deals, signed agreements/NDAs, the last time you talked, the current status, and the next step — all in one place. Last-touch and agreement status update themselves from the email you route to exosmc@proton.me and the document archive.

Contacts — built automatically from your email

The 👥 Contacts tab captures everyone you correspond with — but only from the emails you BCC or forward to exosmc@proton.me (Step 4). It pulls name, company, last contact, and how many times you have exchanged messages. A flag shows whether they are already in the investor CRM, so nobody slips through the cracks. Use the "untracked only" toggle to see who is new. It grows on its own from your routed email — nothing to enter by hand — and it never reads your mailbox, only what you send to that address.

Your daily habit

Two things, and Mission Control does the rest:

  • As you work: on the emails you have confirmed are free of CUI and ITAR, add exosmc@proton.me (BCC to capture, or forward with @Exobot to task it). That keeps your tasks, contacts, and record current with zero data entry — the system is fed by the work you are already doing.
  • Once a day: open the board, click My Tasks, move anything that progressed, and check your Decisions tab for anything waiting on your approval.

That is it — there is no separate work-management system to keep up. Your email is the system.
