🔴 Your Daily Workflow — Mission Control First

Mission Control is the #1 task of your day. Open it before your inbox and let your board — not your email — decide what you work on. Email is the support tool for Mission Control, not the other way around. The order that keeps work flowing: Decisions → Tasks → Email.

  1. Clear your Decisions first — don't be the bottleneck. Anything waiting on your answer or approval is likely blocking a teammate or one of your own tasks. Decisions are fast (a yes/no) and high-leverage, so clear your Decisions queue before anything else — that keeps work flowing for everyone.
  2. Then work your Tasks board, top-down. Start with the red-glowing critical items (overdue + top priority), then everything else in Mission Score order. Clearing decisions first often re-ranks or unblocks tasks, so your board is accurate by the time you get here.
  3. Use email to execute, not to prioritize. Email is how you communicate and get things done — but it serves the board. Forward or BCC what matters to exosmc@proton.me and it becomes tasks, contacts, and decisions automatically. A full inbox is not your to-do list; your board is.
  4. Close the loop before you log off. Decisions cleared, no red (critical/overdue) items left, and status updates posted. If it's not in Mission Control, it didn't happen.
Rule of thumb: if you're deciding what to do next, look at Mission Control — not your inbox. Clear decisions, then work the board top-down. Email supports the board; the board runs your day.

✅ How to answer a Decision

A Decision is a quick call waiting on you. Each one is usually one of three things:

  • Yes / No — just pick the option.
  • Reassign — if it's not really yours, send it to someone better equipped to answer (use "Not my decision") and it moves to their queue.
  • A custom answer — type your own response in the box.

Make your choice (or type your answer) before you click Submit. You can line up answers to several decisions and submit them together.

What Mission Control is

Think of it as one shared online board for everything we are working on: who is doing what, what is due, what is waiting on a decision, and where the documents live. Instead of work scattered across email, Teamwork, and people's heads, it all lives in one place everyone can see. You open it in a web browser. It is replacing Teamwork.

The big idea — you do not maintain it by hand. You keep working in email exactly as you do now. By forwarding or BCC'ing the right messages to one address (exosmc@proton.me), your everyday communication becomes the work management: Mission Control turns it into tasks, contacts, decisions, and status updates for you. No separate system to keep up, no data entry.

📧 Email a task straight to Mission Control

You can hand work to Mission Control by email — send it To: exosmc@pm.me (or exosmc@proton.me). Putting it in the To: line is the signal to act — even without any "@Exobot" keyword, it becomes a task and Exo works it.

  • To: exosmc = "do this / track this." Exo creates or updates a task (assigned to you), acts on it, and emails you back. Anything external or sensitive is drafted for your approval as a Decision — never auto-sent.
  • CC / BCC exosmc = passive. It's just archived and tracked — copying it on a thread does not trigger any action.
  • Only your work email counts. Sends are authenticated (must be a Mission Control user, from your verified address), so spoofed or outside mail is ignored.
Rule of thumb: To: exosmc when you want Exo to do or track something; CC/BCC exosmc when you just want it on the record. Use subject [DOC] to file a document, or @Exobot <instruction> anywhere in a forwarded email to give a specific command.

🔐 Your access — what you'll see

Mission Control shows different things to different people based on your role. If a menu, page, or record isn't visible to you, that's your access level by design — not a bug. Ask John or Lizi if your role needs to change.

  • Admin — everything, plus user and system settings.
  • Manager — the full board, CRM, deals, and reports.
  • Member — the task board, CRM, contacts, relationship dossiers, and the Procedures library. Deals, financials, and admin settings are hidden.
  • Early adopter — a focused preview: the task board for one assigned project only (no CRM, deals, or documents).

Scoped views. Some accounts are also scoped to a segment of the data. For example, an international account sees only Non-CONUS (international) contacts in the CRM — domestic contacts are filtered out. That narrowing is intentional. In the CRM you can use the Region filter (All / Non-CONUS / CONUS) to switch between segments if your role allows it, and a contact can be added to the international segment by tagging it non-conus.

Always walled off for everyone: CUI and ITAR-controlled material never lives in Mission Control — it stays in the secure GCC High / SharePoint environment. Feed reports in by emailing them to exosmc@proton.me; controlled content is screened out automatically.

💬 How to use Ask Exobot (the chat window)

Click the 💬 button in the bottom-right corner — it's on every Mission Control page. Type your question and press Enter. Ask it anything, or tell it to do things: "add a task to…", "run DD on this company", "find the latest MDB MNDA", "meeting prep for Tuesday's review", "how do I…?" Your chat is private to you.

  • It follows you across pages. Ask a question, then keep working — open Tasks, Decisions, a document, whatever. The conversation and the answer come with you; you won't lose it by navigating away. (Before, moving to another page dropped the question — that's fixed.)
  • Ask several things at once. Kick off a research question, and while it's working, ask a second one — they run in parallel, so you can work a second task instead of waiting. Each in-progress question shows a "…working: …" line, and each answer drops in when it's ready. Up to 4 at a time.
  • You'll know when an answer lands. If you've closed the chat, a small red badge appears on the 💬 button when a reply comes in.
  • Replies take a few seconds to a minute — it's the full assistant doing real work, not a canned bot. Longer research takes longer, which is exactly why you can start a second question while you wait.
  • Start fresh anytime with the 🗑 button in the chat header to clear the conversation.
💡 Think of it like running several task channels at once: fire off the slow research, then keep moving — the answers catch up to you wherever you are on the board.

⚠️ Compliance — no CUI or ITAR data

At this time, do not attach documents to Mission Control, and do not BCC or forward emails into it, that contain CUI (Controlled Unclassified Information) or ITAR / EAR-controlled data. That content lives only in its designated SharePoint folder. When in doubt, leave it out and keep it in SharePoint.

Backup, not a substitute for your judgment: as a safety net, Mission Control automatically screens everything you route to exosmc@proton.me for ITAR / EAR / CUI indicators. If something trips the screen, it is held — not filed, not acted on. You, the person who routed it, then get a Decision in your own Decisions tab asking you to confirm whether the hold was valid — so you always know it happened and you make the call (controlled content is never auto-released). But you are the first line of defense: the screen catches the obvious, not everything, so never rely on it to make the call for you.

Where to start each day — Decisions first, then Tasks

Your work lives in two places: the Decisions tab and the Tasks board. Work them in that order.

  • 1) Start in Decisions. These are the quick yes/no calls that need you — approvals, sign-offs, "send this / hold that." Each is usually a 30-second answer, and answering it unblocks work: most decisions are tied to a task that is paused waiting on your call, so clearing the Decisions inbox releases the most work for the least effort. Do these first, every morning.
  • 2) Then work your Tasks. With the gates cleared, go to your board and work top-down — it is already sorted HIGH priority first, then soonest due date, so the top card is what to do next. Drag cards along as you go; that is your status update.
Rule of thumb: Decisions unblock, Tasks execute. A task sitting in Blocked almost always has a matching Decision waiting on you — answer it and the task frees up. Clear Decisions to zero, then spend your day in Tasks.

Step 4 — The engine: your email does the work management for you

This is the heart of it. You do not log tasks or keep a system up to date by hand — your email is the input. On your normal work emails, add exosmc@proton.me. Mission Control reads who you are and what the message is about and does the filing for you — no task numbers, no codes, nothing to look up.

Why BCC'ing Exo matters — two payoffs. First, it is how your board stays current for you: every message you route updates the right task, contact, and status with zero data entry, so nothing falls through the cracks. Second, it is how Exobot learns your world — your relationships, your priorities, how you actually work. The more you loop it in, the sharper its help gets and the more it can take off your plate. Routing your email is what turns Exobot into a genuinely better assistant for you.

One address, two very different jobs — this is the part that trips people up, so read it carefully:

  • BCC / CC / forward it with NO @Exobot → it CAPTURES (it does not create a task). The people on the email are added to Contacts, and if the message matches a task that already exists, it is filed under that task and the status is updated. This is also how a send you make gets logged — e.g. a cold outreach auto-closes, or a warm follow-up advances. What it will not do is make a new task appear: if there is no existing task to match, nothing new shows up on the board. That is expected behavior, not a malfunction.
  • Put @Exobot + a plain-English instruction at the top → it ACTS. This is the only way to create a task, reassign one, draft a reply, or pull research. Example: @Exobot create a task to order harness connectors, assign to me, due Friday. Simply CC'ing or forwarding to the address without @Exobot will not create anything — you must include @Exobot in the body (or start the subject with [EXO]).
📩 BCC vs CC — which to use. Use BCC to loop Exo in: it captures the copy without revealing exosmc@proton.me to the person you emailed. CC works identically for Exo, but it shows that address to your recipient — so for any outside correspondence, prefer BCC. Either way, remember: capturing a copy is not the same as creating a task — for a task, you still need @Exobot.
⚠️ Forward inline, not "as an attachment." When forwarding from Outlook, use the normal Forward so the original lands in the message body — not "Forward as Attachment" (an "Outlook Item"). The attachment form wraps the message in a Microsoft-proprietary format that Proton can reject, so it may bounce back as undeliverable. If a forward ever bounces, resend it inline.
🔒 Compliance is in your hands. Only send messages you have confirmed are free of CUI and ITAR. Nothing enters Mission Control unless you send it to exosmc@proton.me — it does not read your mailbox, so you decide exactly what is captured. When in doubt, keep it in SharePoint and do not forward it.

Ask Exobot to do it — just @Exobot

Need something done? Type @Exobot and then, in plain English, the task. That is the entire trigger — everything you write after @Exobot is the instruction it carries out. No commands, no codes, no format.

  • Examples: @Exobot add a task to follow up with Marlett on Friday · @Exobot pull the latest on the AEDC solicitation and summarize it · @Exobot draft a reply to this email for my review.
  • It works the same way in chat and in email — in email, forward the message to exosmc@proton.me with @Exobot plus your instruction at the top (same address as Step 4).
  • The guardrail: Exobot does safe, internal work directly — creating and updating tasks, research, drafts, summaries, reminders — and any task or document it makes for you is assigned and attached to you. Anything external or sensitive — sending emails, government or contract submissions, financial or legal actions — it prepares and adds to your own Decisions matrix in Mission Control for your review and approval. It never sends those on its own, and approvals for your work come to you, not to anyone else. When you approve a send, the ready message is emailed to your own inbox for you to send — the system never sends to the outside party for you.
  • Vetting a solicitor or suspicious inbound? Forward it with @Exobot run DD on this. Exo checks it against a shared failed-DD registry of known bad actors and runs diligence — a known cold-broker/advance-fee/scam is flagged instantly, and any new one it catches is added so the whole team is protected the next time that outfit emails anyone. You do not need to forward these to Jon — Exo handles the vetting and only surfaces what's real.
